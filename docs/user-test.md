## What we are testing
The hypothesis: *when decisions depend on private limits, answering privately and seeing one
unattributed plan makes the decision easier than deciding by talking it through.* We do **not** know this yet.

## Who
2-3 real groups of 3+ adults who actually need to decide something together (siblings sharing a task,
friends planning a weekend or trip). Adults only; everyone consents; nobody is deceived.
Use groups of three or more: with two people the other can infer your number (a stated limit).

## Protocol (about 20 minutes per group)
1. Explain in one minute what Huddle does and does not do. Say the limits aloud: answers are private
   and never attributed, but it is not cryptographic, and with two people a number can be inferred.
2. **Before:** each person privately rates (1-5) "How stressful does deciding this feel?". Use a paper slip or their own phone.
3. Run a round in the simulator (voice/typed at the device, remote person via link). Time from "start" to
   "everyone has seen the plan".
4. **After:** each person privately rates (1-5) "How stressful was deciding?" and "How comfortable are you with the plan?" (the in-app check-in does the second).
5. One open question each: "What, if anything, was awkward or unclear?" Record one quote per person, with permission.
6. Optional next day: the in-app check-in again.
7. Note anything that broke.
